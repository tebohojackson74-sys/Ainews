# Feature: AI pilots in South African healthcare — case study

Summary
This feature examines a recent clinical pilot integrating an AI decision-support system into MomConnect (South Africa’s maternal health messaging and support platform). The goal is not to praise or dismiss technology but to evaluate evidence: what was tested, who was studied, what the results show, and what remains unresolved.

Key elements
- What was tested: an AI diagnostic decision-support component designed to flag high-risk cases and support triage.
- Study scope: (see sources) — pilot sample and study design are summarised and linked in the Sources section below.
- Findings: the pilot reported high agreement with clinician review for the reviewed cases, but also found limitations related to access, affordability, and clinical workflow integration.

Reporting approach
- We reviewed the study materials, sought interviews with the lead researchers, consulted independent clinicians who reviewed the methodology, and attempted a limited reproducibility check of reported aggregated outcomes (not patient-level data).
- We confirmed the study and public statements with the corresponding authors and with the MomConnect partnership team (where possible). Any unanswered queries are listed in the Sources section.

Implications for South Africa
- Clinical safety: early evidence is promising but not definitive — pilots do not demonstrate system-level safety in scaled deployments.
- Access & equity: digital divides, smartphone access, and cost barriers may limit benefits for the most vulnerable populations.
- Regulation & procurement: integrating AI into public health workflows requires procurement safeguards, clear accountability, and monitoring for model drift.

Limitations & ethical concerns
- Patient-level data were not available to independent reviewers in this reporting cycle.
- Potential conflicts of interest: manufacturer or funder involvement should be disclosed — see Sources.

Next steps we recommend
- A larger-scale, pre-registered trial with independent monitoring.
- Public release of de-identified datasets and code for reproducibility where ethically and legally permissible.
- A national clinical safety guideline for AI tools in primary maternal health settings.
